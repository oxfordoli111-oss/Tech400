A mission to raise the orbit of NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory is poised for launch from Kwajalein Atoll, part of the Republic of the Marshall Islands in the South Pacific Ocean, with partners Katalyst Space and Northrop Grumman. Swift launched in November 2004 to study gamma-ray bursts, the most powerful explosions in the universe, along with other cosmic objects and events. Earth's atmosphere creates drag on all spacecraft in low Earth orbit, gradually reducing their altitudes, and a recent bout of increased solar activity accelerated this effect on Swift. Rather than allowing Swift to re-enter the atmosphere, NASA is using the opportunity to advance the U.S. commercial satellite servicing industry. In September 2025, the agency contracted Katalyst Space to attempt to boost the observatory, giving the company less than one year to design, build, test and launch a spacecraft to meet, grab and lift Swift back to nearly its original orbit. The LINK spacecraft weighs about 880 pounds and stands about 5 feet tall, roughly a third of Swift's size, with solar panels powering three ion thrusters and a trio of robotic arms.
