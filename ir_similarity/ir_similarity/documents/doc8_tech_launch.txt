The Federal Communications Commission took two major steps this week to advance next-generation connectivity, establishing a regulatory framework for fast connections directly from smartphones and other devices to high-speed satellites. The FCC acted to create the regulatory environment that will allow the growing market for direct-to-device services, connectivity provided directly to a smartphone or device from next-generation satellites, to develop and flourish throughout the United States. These technologies, which promise an end to cellular dead zones and enhanced competition, are drawing widespread interest, innovation, and investment; in the last eighteen months alone, there has been more than $28 billion in deal flow across at least 130 megahertz of spectrum that will be used for this purpose. The FCC's Space Bureau granted AST SpaceMobile a permanent license to operate the company's foundational satellite system, authorizing it to deploy a constellation of 248 satellites throughout the United States, working in partnership with AT&T, Verizon, and FirstNet.
